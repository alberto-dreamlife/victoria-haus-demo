Victoria Drive at East 23rd

A small building that knows its street.

Four homes on a single corner lot in Kensington-Cedar Cottage, the kind of quiet, tree-lined block that made this part of Vancouver worth living in. Two blocks from Trout Lake, ten minutes from The Drive, and close enough to the 20 that you can leave the car behind.

The entry walk
The genkan

You take your shoes off here.

Every home opens into a genkan, the recessed entry vestibule of a Japanese house, where the street stops and the home begins. It is a small idea, and it changes how the whole ground floor works: coats, boots and bicycles have somewhere to live, and the living room stays a living room.

Beyond it, the ground floor runs the full depth of the home: kitchen, dining and living in one uninterrupted line, opening onto a private garden patio.

The homes

Four homes. Four trees.

The four are the same size and the same plan. What separates them is where they sit on the lot: which street they face, where the light comes from, and how the garden falls. Each is named for a tree that grows here.

The neighbourhood

Two blocks to the lake.

Trout Lake sits at the top of the street: swimming in July, the Saturday farmers market year-round, and the community centre and rink beside it. Victoria Drive itself carries the bakeries, the produce shops and the coffee. Commercial Drive is ten minutes west on foot.
